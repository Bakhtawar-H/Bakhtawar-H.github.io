Content strategy for the ISU Foundation Relations site

An internal SharePoint site that helps Iowa State faculty and staff find private foundation funding. I shaped it from an open-ended request into a site map and a working site.

Context
Graduate Research Administrative Assistant, Iowa State University Foundation
Timeline
Fall 2026 to present
My role
Content strategy, information architecture, site map, site build
Status
In progress, content under review

The challenge

When I joined the Foundation in Fall 2026, the team knew they wanted a site where faculty and staff could learn what Foundation Relations does, but there was no plan for what it should contain or how it should be organized. My job was to turn that idea into a structure people could navigate.

What I did

I worked from the team's project workbook, which listed the content they had in mind, and used Claude as a drafting partner to plan the site map and page layouts. After several rounds of review with the team, I built the site in SharePoint.

Key decisions

  • Route by audience, then share. The home page sends visitors to Research-Focused or Non-Research-Focused Funding. Both pages then link into one shared set of toolkits. I noticed that the two audience tabs in the workbook listed the same 23 items in the same order, so that guidance is written once instead of twice.
  • Every page within two clicks of the main navigation.
  • Long guidance gets its own pages. Pre-Award and Post-Award guidance is reference material that both audiences need, so each became a shared page.
  • One opportunities list. A single Microsoft List feeds the full opportunities page and filtered previews elsewhere.
  • Forms are buttons, not pages. “Connect with us” and “Have a project idea?” open Microsoft Forms from the header and landing pages.
  • Order follows what visitors need. On the home page, the explanation and the two funding paths come first, then upcoming deadlines (the most common reason to come back), and update-heavy sections last, so the page looks finished before those launch.
  • A phased launch. Nine pages and two forms at launch, with news, events and faculty visibility planned for later.

Outcome

The site is built, and the team is reviewing its content before launch. Because it sits on Iowa State's internal SharePoint, it isn't publicly viewable.

This project is information architecture from the build side: starting with no brief, I had to define the audiences, decide what belongs on each page and give every piece of content one home.
