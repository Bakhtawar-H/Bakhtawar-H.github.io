Website recovery audit for the Village of Potsdam

A content inventory and recovery plan for a municipal website, including a comparison of three ways to extract text from page screenshots.

Context
Graduate course project, Fall 2026
Team
Group project, three members
My role
AI text extraction, content modeling, recovery and remediation plan, recovery memo, future recommendations, README
Tools
Claude, Tesseract, Python EasyOCR, Excel, GitHub

The challenge

The Village of Potsdam's website needed to be recovered and rebuilt. Working from screenshots of a sample of pages, our team had to inventory the content, work out how much could be recovered automatically, and recommend what the new site should fix.

My contributions

  • AI text extraction. I extracted page text with Claude, sharing two screenshots at a time and saving one text file per page.
  • Content modeling. For each page I recorded the category, page type, heading structure, content types and the best extraction method.
  • Recovery and remediation plan. I wrote the plan: what can be recovered automatically, what needs human checking, what to fix in the rebuild and how to keep a larger project manageable.
  • Documentation. I wrote the recovery memo to the Village of Potsdam, recorded our recommendations for the future site, and created the project README in the team's GitHub repository.

The whole team built the content inventory, compared the tools, analyzed the results and documented the issues together.

Comparing three extraction methods

MethodWhat we found
Claude (image to text)No content errors in my check. It moved the menus below the page text and added its own labels, such as “Site Navigation” and “Side Menu”.
Tesseract and EasyOCRBullets read as stray symbols, missing periods in email addresses, misspellings, garbled logo text, menu items merged into body text and missing footer text.

The difference matters most for contact details: a single missing period makes an email address useless. Even with the best method, we recommended that a person check every email, phone number and address.

What the audit found

  • The Village Board Agendas page is headed “Minutes Entries”, and its main list stops at 2014 while newer years hide in links at the bottom.
  • Most of the site sits in one long list of top-level sections, with no grouping.
  • Page names are inconsistent, for example “Zoning Board of Appeals” and “Zoning Board”.
  • Several topics appear twice under different menus, such as Police and Police Department, or Museum and Museum Board.
  • The Trustees page sends readers to the “NEWS” tab for the calendar, even though the site has a Calendars button.

The recovery plan

I summarized the plan in a memo to the Village. The menus and footer repeat on every page, so they only need recovering once. Recovery should start with contact details and resident services (Public Works, Planning and Development, Clerk, Police, payments and forms), then board pages, then meeting archives.

For a larger project I proposed a traceable workflow: keep raw and corrected text in separate folders, log every error in a spreadsheet, track duplicate content and what can be reused, revise the site map, and group pages into clearer sections.

Recommendations for the future site

  • Departments: replace the long list of links with a dropdown menu that includes every department and office, so people can go straight to the page they need.
  • Parking tickets: confirm the official payment provider and link, give clear payment instructions, and make the service easy to find.
  • Overall structure: work with a web developer on a layout with a clear menu hierarchy and less repeated content across pages.

This audit connects to my research on how people give AI context. The most accurate tool still needed a human to check its output, and the decisions about structure were ours to make.
